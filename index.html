2. Climate and drought twin

Core indicators

  • Rainfall anomaly and Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI)
  • Temperature anomaly and heat indicators
  • Consecutive dry days and seasonal onset
  • Soil-moisture anomaly
  • Evapotranspiration and evaporative demand

Climate anomaly formulation

Calculate departures from a documented reference climatology:

Absolute anomalyAₜ = Xₜ − X̄baseline
Standardized anomalyZₜ = (Xₜ − μ) / σ

Use consistent baseline periods and account for seasonality, missing data and sensor changes.

3. AI climate prediction

  • XGBoost / LightGBM for tabular predictors
  • LSTM / TFT for temporal sequences
  • Ensembles for forecast combination
  • Climatology and persistence baselines

Evaluate skill by lead time, season and region. Report uncertainty, not only deterministic forecasts.

4. Agricultural productivity and exposure

Combine cropland masks with rainfall, temperature, soil moisture and vegetation indices.

  • NDVI/EVI and crop-condition anomalies
  • Planting-season rainfall and dry spells
  • Crop-area exposure by country or catchment
  • Yield statistics where available

Ecosystem exposure

Assess vegetation stress and water-related ecosystem vulnerability.

  • Wetland and riparian condition
  • Vegetation productivity anomalies
  • Fire-weather context
  • Land-cover and habitat exposure

Exposure is not impact

Satellite-derived exposure identifies where climate stress overlaps with a land-cover class. Productivity impacts require crop calendars, crop type, management, ground observations and yield data.

5. Water-security and hydrological twin

Represent catchment water balance over a defined spatial and temporal boundary.

Conceptual water balanceP − ET = Q + ΔS + ε

P = precipitation; ET = evapotranspiration; Q = runoff/drainage across the selected boundary; ΔS = change in storage; ε = residual terms and errors.

  • Rainfall and ET anomalies
  • Surface and subsurface runoff
  • Reservoir storage and river-flow observations
  • Water demand and supply where available

Water-security decision outputs

  • Catchments with persistent rainfall deficits
  • Potential inflow and storage pressure
  • Areas with declining soil-water availability
  • Priority monitoring locations
  • Scenario comparison for demand management
P − ET is a climatic water-availability proxy. It is not equivalent to streamflow, sustainable yield or groundwater recharge.

6. Groundwater recharge and vulnerability

Integrate climate forcing with land-surface and subsurface processes. Use JULES soil-moisture and subsurface-runoff outputs alongside borehole levels, hydrogeology and recharge estimates where available.

Initial vulnerability screening indexGRVI = 0.35RS + 0.30SMS + 0.20WS + 0.15VS

RS = rainfall stress; SMS = soil-moisture stress; WS = water stress; VS = vegetation stress. Weights are proposed screening assumptions, not universal calibrated coefficients.

Validation and safeguards

  • Compare modelled soil moisture and runoff with observations.
  • Use borehole hydrographs and aquifer characteristics.
  • Distinguish recharge, groundwater storage change and abstraction.
  • Test sensitivity to index weights and thresholds.
  • Report GRVI as vulnerability screening, not measured recharge.
GRACE observes total terrestrial water-storage changes at coarse spatial scales; it cannot independently resolve local aquifer recharge. P − ET is not a direct recharge measurement.

9. Model validation

  • Temporal holdout and independent test years
  • Spatial holdout across catchments/countries
  • Climatology and persistence baselines
  • Bias, RMSE, MAE, correlation and skill
  • Uncertainty and ensemble spread

Reproducibility

  • Versioned datasets and processing scripts
  • Documented reference periods and units
  • Model configuration and parameter tracking
  • Saved scenario inputs and outputs
  • Data provenance and quality flags

Scientific contributions

  • Multi-source EO database
  • Validated climate-hydrology indicators
  • AI and process-based model integration
  • Agricultural and ecosystem exposure
  • Groundwater vulnerability scenarios
  • Reproducible adaptation decision support
